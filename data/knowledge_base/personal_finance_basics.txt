PERSONAL FINANCE BASICS

1. What is Personal Finance?
Personal finance is the process of managing income, expenses, savings,
investments, debt, and financial goals. Good financial management helps
individuals maintain financial stability and prepare for future needs.

2. Budgeting
A budget is a plan for allocating income toward expenses, savings, and
financial goals.

A popular budgeting method is the 50/30/20 rule:
- 50% of income for needs such as rent, groceries, and utilities.
- 30% for wants such as entertainment and shopping.
- 20% for savings and debt repayment.

This is a guideline, not a strict rule. Individuals should adjust the
allocation according to their income and circumstances.

3. Emergency Fund
An emergency fund is money reserved for unexpected expenses such as
medical emergencies, job loss, or urgent repairs.

A common starting target is three to six months of essential living
expenses. People with irregular income or dependants may need a larger
reserve.

Emergency savings should generally be accessible and kept separate
from everyday spending money.

4. Saving Money
Saving means setting aside a portion of income for future use.
Useful practices include:
- Track monthly income and expenses.
- Set realistic savings targets.
- Automate regular savings where possible.
- Reduce unnecessary recurring expenses.
- Review progress every month.

5. Managing Debt
Debt management involves controlling borrowing and repaying outstanding
balances responsibly.

Good practices include:
- Understand the interest rate and repayment terms.
- Pay required instalments on time.
- Prioritise expensive debt where practical.
- Avoid borrowing beyond repayment capacity.
- Maintain a record of outstanding liabilities.

6. Financial Goals
Financial goals should be specific, measurable, realistic, and
time-bound.

Examples include:
- Building an emergency fund.
- Saving for education.
- Purchasing a vehicle.
- Repaying a loan.
- Saving for retirement.

A financial goal should have a target amount, current savings, and
a target completion date.

7. Important Disclaimer
This document provides general financial education, not personalised
investment, legal, or tax advice. Financial decisions should consider
individual circumstances, risk tolerance, and applicable regulations.
