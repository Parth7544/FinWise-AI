SAVINGS AND INVESTMENTS

1. Difference Between Saving and Investing
Saving means keeping money aside for future needs, usually with low risk
and easy access.

Investing means putting money into financial assets with the expectation
of earning returns over time. Investments involve varying levels of risk.

2. Fixed Deposits (FD)
A fixed deposit allows an individual to deposit a lump sum for a
specified period at an agreed interest rate.

Important factors:
- Deposit amount.
- Interest rate.
- Investment duration.
- Compounding frequency.
- Premature withdrawal conditions.

Returns depend on the bank's terms and applicable interest rates.

3. Recurring Deposits (RD)
A recurring deposit allows an individual to deposit a fixed amount
regularly, generally every month, for a specified period.

It can help develop disciplined saving habits.

4. Systematic Investment Plans (SIP)
An SIP allows an investor to invest a fixed amount at regular intervals
in a mutual fund.

Important considerations:
- Investment duration.
- Market performance.
- Fund expenses.
- Risk tolerance.
- Investment objectives.

SIP returns are not guaranteed because mutual fund investments are
subject to market risks.

5. Investment Risk
Investment risk refers to the possibility that actual returns may be
lower than expected or that an investor may lose money.

Common risk factors include:
- Market fluctuations.
- Inflation.
- Interest rate changes.
- Credit risk.
- Liquidity risk.

6. Compound Interest
Compound interest is calculated on the initial principal and accumulated
interest.

The compound amount can be calculated using:

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

Where:
P = Principal amount
r = Annual interest rate in decimal form
n = Number of compounding periods per year
t = Number of years

7. Diversification
Diversification means spreading investments across different assets
to reduce dependence on a single investment.

Diversification does not eliminate all investment risks.

8. Financial Disclaimer
Investment calculators provide mathematical estimates. Actual returns
depend on product terms, market conditions, taxes, fees, and other
factors. Past performance does not guarantee future returns.
