FINANCIAL PLANNING AND RISK MANAGEMENT

1. Financial Planning
Financial planning is the process of setting financial goals and
creating a strategy to achieve them.

It includes:
- Managing income and expenses.
- Building savings.
- Managing debt.
- Planning investments.
- Preparing for emergencies.
- Monitoring financial progress.

2. Net Worth
Net worth represents the difference between total assets and total
liabilities.

Net Worth = Total Assets - Total Liabilities

Assets may include:
- Cash and bank balances.
- Fixed deposits.
- Investments.
- Property.
- Vehicles.

Liabilities may include:
- Personal loans.
- Home loans.
- Credit card balances.
- Other outstanding debts.

3. Financial Health
Financial health describes how effectively an individual manages
financial responsibilities and prepares for future needs.

Useful indicators include:
- Savings rate.
- Debt-to-income ratio.
- Emergency fund coverage.
- Expense-to-income ratio.
- Ability to meet financial goals.

4. Savings Rate
Savings rate measures the percentage of income saved.

Savings Rate = (Monthly Savings / Monthly Income) × 100

A higher savings rate can improve financial resilience, provided
essential needs and debt obligations are also managed responsibly.

5. Debt-to-Income Ratio
Debt-to-income ratio compares monthly debt payments with gross monthly
income.

DTI = (Monthly Debt Payments / Gross Monthly Income) × 100

A high ratio may indicate that a large portion of income is committed
to debt repayment.

6. Financial Risk Management
Financial risk management involves identifying and reducing possible
financial threats.

Examples include:
- Unexpected medical expenses.
- Loss of employment.
- Rising interest rates.
- Investment losses.
- Unexpected household expenses.

7. Financial Goal Prioritisation
Goals can be prioritised according to:
- Urgency.
- Importance.
- Target amount.
- Available savings.
- Time remaining.
- Financial consequences of delay.

Essential goals and emergency reserves generally deserve attention
before discretionary purchases.

8. Reviewing Financial Progress
Financial plans should be reviewed regularly.

A monthly review can examine:
- Income and spending.
- Savings progress.
- Outstanding debt.
- Investment contributions.
- Goal milestones.
- Changes in financial circumstances.

9. Financial Disclaimer
Financial health indicators are planning tools, not guarantees of
financial security. Recommendations should consider individual
circumstances and should not be treated as professional financial,
investment, legal, or tax advice.
